    Summary

    Reaction schemes

    Reaction Drawing

    Separate reactants with a “+”, and enter products in the matching field. Names and SMILES can be mixed freely.

    ⟶
    💡 Put the number of moles in front of a species: 2 H2 + O2, 2H2O, or fractional 1/2 O2. Names, formulas, SMILES, and InChI all work seamlessly.
    How to write an equation - syntax, examples, and what you get back

    1. The shape of an equation

    Put the reactants in the left box and the products in the right one. Separate species with +. The arrow is the gap between the two boxes - you do not type it. 

    Reactants:  CH4 + 2 O2
Products:   CO2 + 2 H2O

    2. Labels above and below the arrow

    Use Above arrow and Below arrow for catalysts, solvents, temperature, pressure, light, or other reaction conditions. The arrow will expand to fit the longer label. Type ^ immediately before a superscript segment; the marker itself is removed and the superscript ends at the next space. Example: H^+ displays H+, and SO4^2- displays the charge as superscript.

    2. Number of moles (stoichiometric coefficient)

    Write the number in front of the species. Three forms are accepted:

    • 2 H2O - a number, a space, then the species.
    • 2H2O - glued to a formula. Only read as a coefficient when what follows is a bare formula, so 2-butanone, 1H-pyrrole and 13C-methanol keep their names intact.
    • 1/2 O2 or 0.5 O2 - a fraction, as thermochemistry is usually written.

    A species with no number in front of it counts as 1.

    3. Naming a species

    Any of these work, and they can be mixed in the same equation:

    • Name-water, acetic acid, benzene (looked up in PubChem).
    • Formula-H2O, CO2, NaCl.
    • SMILES-CCO, c1ccccc1, CC(=O)O. Best for anything with a specific structure.
    • InChI - paste it starting with InChI=.

    A few strings are both a valid SMILES and a common formula - CO is methanol as SMILES and carbon monoxide as a formula. Those are read as the formula and the page tells you which reading it used, so you can switch to an unambiguous SMILES if it guessed the other way.

    4. What gets drawn, and what gets written

    Small unambiguous species are written as formulas - O2, H2O, CO2, NH3 - because a skeletal drawing of O2 is two letters and a line, taking the width of a benzene ring to say less. Everything else is drawn as a structure. Ethanol is always drawn, never written as C2H6O: dimethyl ether has that same formula, so writing it would throw away exactly what the drawing is for. Untick Write small molecules as formulas to draw every species instead.

    5. The balance check

    After drawing, every element and the total charge are counted on both sides using your coefficients. If they do not match you are told which element is in excess and on which side. The scheme is still drawn - a mechanism step or a fragment is legitimately unbalanced, and the problem this guards against is not noticing.

    6. The downloaded .rxn file

    You get an MDL RXN V2000 file, which is the format ChemDraw, MarvinSketch, Avogadro and RDKit all import. That format has no field for a coefficient, so the convention every reader understands is used instead: the structure appears once per unit. 2 H2O puts two water molecules in the file, and ChemDraw will show two of them rather than the text “2 H2O”. The stoichiometry is there and machine-readable, just not as a numeral. A fractional coefficient cannot be written at all, so all coefficients are multiplied up to whole numbers - the ratios are unchanged, and the factor used is reported.

    The file is checked before you are offered it: the $RXN header, the component counts matching the blocks that follow, and a M  END terminator on every structure. The badge under the drawing reports that check.

    7. A worked example

    The complete combustion of benzene, with the coefficients balanced:

    Reactants:  2 benzene + 15 O2
Products:   12 CO2 + 6 H2O

    Benzene is drawn as a ring; O2, CO2 and H2O are written as formulas; the badge confirms the equation balances; and the .rxn file contains 2 benzenes, 15 oxygens, 12 carbon dioxides and 6 waters - 35 structures in total.
